# Privacy, Security and Compliance of Personalized, Memory-Enabled Chatbots: ChatGPT (2024 to Oct 2026) as Case Study, with Lessons for a B2B ICP / Lead-Qualification Bot

Research date: 2026-10-23. Not legal advice. Jurisdiction is flagged on each regulatory point. Some OpenAI primary pages (help.openai.com, openai.com/enterprise-privacy, openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool in this session, so the ChatGPT data-control details below rely on OpenAI-adjacent secondary sources. Those points are marked and should be checked against OpenAI's pages before anyone quotes them externally.

## 1. ChatGPT data controls: training opt-out, Temporary Chat, deletion, memory, and Enterprise / Team / API (ZDR) differences

### Takeaway
ChatGPT splits its controls by tier. Consumer plans train on content by default, with a per-user opt-out. Business, Enterprise and API data is not used for training by default. Deletion and Temporary Chat both run on a "within 30 days" clock. Memory is stored separately from chats, so deleting a chat does not delete what memory learned from it. The 2025 court hold showed that a "30-day" promise only holds until a legal hold overrides it. Enterprise, Edu and Zero Data Retention (ZDR) customers were the main groups carved out of that hold.

### Cited Findings
- **Temporary Chat (all ChatGPT tiers).** Temporary Chat "is not used to improve models", does not create or update memories, and "a copy may be kept for up to 30 days for safety purposes" — [WitnessAI, AI Data Retention (2026)](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/), citing [OpenAI Help: Temporary chat](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8914046-temporary-chat-in-chatgpt) (OpenAI page not directly fetchable in this session).
- **Temporary Chat (Enterprise).** On Enterprise, temporary chats "also flow into the Compliance API for 30 days" — [WitnessAI, AI Data Retention](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/).
- **Memory vs. deletion.** "Saved memories can persist separately from an individual conversation." Deleting a chat may therefore leave information that memory carried forward — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data? (Aug 29, 2026)](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **Reference chat history.** Turning off "Reference chat history" schedules the information remembered from past chats for deletion within 30 days. The source conversations stay in history until the user deletes them separately — [WitnessAI, AI Data Retention](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/).
- **Consumer training opt-out.** On Free, Plus and Pro, "content may be used for model training by default unless the user opts out" — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data?](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/). The opt-out only covers future conversations, and data already used for training stays in the training set — [WitnessAI, AI Data Retention](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/).
- **Consumer deletion.** Deleted chats are "scheduled for permanent removal within the 30-day deletion window", unless the data has been de-identified or a legal obligation requires longer retention — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data?](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **Business / Enterprise.** "Business-tier data isn't used to train models by default." "Enterprise admins control retention duration for their workspace." Enterprise customers can get a data processing agreement (DPA) — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data?](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **API.** Standard API inputs and outputs are kept for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring. Zero Data Retention is available for eligible endpoints and needs OpenAI's approval — [WitnessAI, AI Data Retention](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/). Stateful API features keep data "until the customer deletes it" and are not ZDR-eligible — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data?](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **Legal holds override deletion.** Litigation holds "often carve out enterprise-tier and Zero Data Retention customers, leaving consumer and standard API users most exposed". OpenAI still holds April–September 2025 data "in a restricted legal-hold environment" — [WitnessAI, Does ChatGPT store your data?](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **Comparator: Anthropic (commercial and API).** Anthropic "automatically delete[s] inputs and outputs on our backend within 30 days of receipt or generation". The exceptions are customer-controlled storage (e.g. the Files API), a ZDR agreement, Usage Policy enforcement, and retention required by law. Flagged content is kept "up to 2 years" and classification scores up to 7 years. Feedback data is kept 5 years — [Anthropic Privacy Center, How long do you store my organization's data? (Jul 1, 2026)](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data).
- **Comparator: Anthropic (consumer).** Since October 8, 2025, consumer users choose whether their chats are used for training. Opting in brings a 5-year retention period. Opting out keeps the 30-day standard — [Anthropic Claude Code data-usage docs](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/claude-code/data-usage) (via search summary); also [Anarlog, Anthropic retention 2026](https://anarlog.so/blog/anthropic-data-retention-policy/).

### Inferences
- For a B2B bot, the useful pattern from ChatGPT is a tiered posture with three properties: (a) no training on customer conversations by default; (b) admin-configurable retention; (c) an "ephemeral" mode that does not write memory. Temporary Chat is ChatGPT's version of (c).
- "Delete chat" and "delete memory" have to be separate, explicit operations, and a "forget me" flow has to cover both. ChatGPT's separation of the two has been widely misunderstood, so the ICP bot's UI should say outright that deleting a session also deletes any derived profile or ICP facts, or offer that as a choice.
- Running the ICP bot on a provider's standard API means a roughly 30-day provider-side retention window (OpenAI abuse monitoring; Anthropic backend deletion) applies unless ZDR is contracted. The bot's privacy notice and DPA sub-processor schedule should state this.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch OpenAI's own help center, enterprise-privacy page or API data-controls page (HTTP 403). The 30-day API abuse-monitoring figure and the ZDR approval requirement come from secondary sources and need re-verification.
- I did not confirm whether ChatGPT Business/Enterprise memory can be centrally disabled by admins, or how it behaves in those tiers.
- One secondary source claimed Anthropic cut standard API log retention to 7 days in September 2025. Anthropic's July 2026 privacy page says 30 days, so I treated the 7-day claim as unverified. A secondary claim of a "mandatory 30-day window for Covered Models even under ZDR" (June 2026) also could not be checked. Anthropic's page only says it "require[s] limited data retention and review" for Covered Models.

## 2. Legal events: NYT v. OpenAI preservation order and 20M-log discovery; Italy Garante €15M fine (and its 2026 annulment); other EU DPA actions; 2026 rulings

### Takeaway
A US discovery order (May 13, 2025) made OpenAI keep "deleted" and temporary ChatGPT output logs for about five months. It was narrowed on October 9, 2025: the going-forward obligation ended as of September 26, 2025. Logs preserved before then, and accounts flagged by the NYT, stay held. In January 2026 Judge Stein upheld production of a 20M de-identified log sample. In July 2026 plaintiffs moved for sanctions over alleged deletions, and I found no ruling on that motion as of October 2026. In the EU, Italy's €15M fine (decision of Nov 2, 2024, announced Dec 2024) was annulled in full by the Court of Rome on March 18, 2026, on jurisdiction alone (one-stop-shop via OpenAI Ireland). The substantive findings were never reviewed and now sit with the Irish DPC.

### Cited Findings
**NYT v. OpenAI (US, S.D.N.Y., consolidated MDL), status: ongoing**
- **The original order.** Magistrate Judge Ona Wang's May 13, 2025 order told OpenAI to "preserve and segregate the output log data due to be deleted" — [quoted in court-document search result; Justia docket PDF](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2023cv11195/612697/559/0.pdf). The order reportedly affected "hundreds of millions of ChatGPT users globally" — [Gizmodo (Oct 2025), citing Ars Technica](https://gizmodo.com/openai-will-stop-saving-users-deleted-posts-2000671374).
- **Narrowing on October 9, 2025.** Judge Ona T. Wang "filed a new order on October 9" ending the requirement to "preserve and segregate all output log data that would otherwise be deleted on a going forward basis". OpenAI no longer had to preserve logs as of **September 26, 2025**, "except for some". OpenAI must still hold data for ChatGPT accounts flagged by the NYT, and the NYT "is allowed to expand the number of flagged users" — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-no-longer-has-to-preserve-all-of-its-chatgpt-data-with-some-exceptions-192422093.html).
- **Already-preserved logs.** These remain accessible to the news plaintiffs, who use them to look for outputs "infringing their articles or attributing misinformation to their publications" — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/openai-will-stop-saving-users-deleted-posts-2000671374).
- **Carve-outs.** Chats from the EEA, Switzerland and the UK were reportedly exempt from the preservation requirement — [Anarlog (secondary), via search summary](https://anarlog.so/blog/chatgpt-data-retention-policy/). Enterprise and Edu customers were exempt from the 2025 order — [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **20M-log discovery, January 5, 2026.** Judge Sidney Stein affirmed Magistrate Judge Wang's order requiring OpenAI to produce 20 million de-identified ChatGPT logs. He rejected OpenAI's proposal to produce only logs tied to plaintiffs' works. He found privacy adequately protected by de-identification plus a protective order, and noted that users had "voluntarily submitted their communications" — [ChatGPT Is Eating the World (Jan 5, 2026)](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/01/05/judge-stein-upholds-disovery-of-20m-chatgpt-chat-logs-of-users/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright). Search summaries describe 20M as about 0.5% of preserved logs, against a plaintiff request of 120M — [Lawyer Monthly (Jan 2026)](https://www.lawyer-monthly.com/2026/01/openai-sdny-discovery-20m-chat-logs-legal-impact/) (secondary; not checked against the docket).
- **Sanctions motion, July 9, 2026.** The NYT and other news plaintiffs moved for sanctions. They allege OpenAI deleted output logs despite the preservation obligation, including about 5% of sampled logs, which were replaced with others, and they seek an adverse-inference instruction and fees. OpenAI says the motion tries to "rewrite history" — [Plaintiffs' memorandum (Ars Technica-hosted PDF)](https://cdn.arstechnica.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NYT-v-OpenAI-Memorandum-of-Law-in-Support-of-Motion-for-Sanctions-Against-OpenAI-7-9-26.pdf); [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/new-york-times-news-outlets-accuse-openai-of-lying-lawsuit-1236805648/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-says-ny-times-seeks-sanctions-due-to-lack-of-evidence). Some outlets say "billions" of logs were deleted, a figure I could not confirm independently — [AI Risk Today](https://www.airisktoday.com/openai-sanctions-motion-chatgpt-logs/).
- **Case schedule.** One tracker reports cross-motions for summary judgment on fair use filed September 4, 2026, with replies due November 6, 2026 — [AI Lawsuit Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/new-york-times-v-openai/) (not verified against the docket; another source had said April 2026).

**Italy Garante v. OpenAI (EU / Italy), status: fine annulled March 2026; possible Cassation appeal**
- **Original decision.** Provvedimento n. 755 of November 2, 2024 imposed a €15,000,000 fine and a six-month public awareness campaign in Italian media. Findings: an unreported data breach (Art. 33) tied to the March 20, 2023 incident; no lawful basis identified for training (Arts. 5(2), 6); inadequate transparency (Arts. 5(1)(a), 12, 13); missing age verification (Arts. 24, 25(1)); and non-compliance with the earlier order n. 114 (Art. 83(5)(e)) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/italian-court-kills-openais-eur15m-fine-and-it-wasnt-even-close/).
- **Annulment.** The Tribunale Ordinario di Roma (R.G. 4785/2025, Judge Damiana Colla) annulled the decision in full on **March 18, 2026**, and published its reasoning on May 28, 2026. It held that the Irish DPC became lead authority once OpenAI Ireland was recognised as OpenAI's EEA establishment on February 15, 2024. Under the one-stop-shop (GDPR Arts. 55–56) the Garante therefore lacked competence, with "the adoption of a final decision" as "the only objective cutoff point". The merits were not examined — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/italian-court-kills-openais-eur15m-fine-and-it-wasnt-even-close/); [Rödl & Partner](https://www.roedl.it/en-gb/it/insights/pages/tech-data-bites/6-26/generative-ai-gdpr-chatgpt-fine-annulled-grounds-competence.aspx); [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/italian-court-scraps-15-million-125926950.html).
- **Further appeal.** One tracker says the Garante could still go to the Court of Cassation, but no filing had been announced as of September 14, 2026 — [Gage Academy EU AI Act enforcement tracker](https://www.gage.academy/tools/eu-ai-act-enforcement-tracker/actions/AIT-2024-0007-garante-openai-fine-annulled-court-of-rome) (via search summary).

**Other EU DPA actions on chatbots**
- **Replika (Italy).** The Garante fined Luka Inc. (Replika) €5M for breaching GDPR Arts. 5(1)(a), 6, 12, 13, 5(1)(c), 24 and 25(1). It found no age verification until February 2, 2023, despite the company's claim that minors were excluded, and said the current system "continues to be deficient". The lawful-basis question across the generative-AI lifecycle was reserved for a separate proceeding — [EDPB news](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/ai-the-italian-supervisory-authority-fines-company-behind-chatbot-replika_ga).
- **noyb hallucination complaint (Norway, EU/EEA).** In March 2025 noyb complained to Datatilsynet on behalf of Arve Hjalmar Holmen, whom ChatGPT had falsely described as a child murderer. noyb asked for deletion of the output, fine-tuning to remove the inaccuracy, and a fine, and argued that a "tiny" disclaimer "clearly isn't enough" under the accuracy and rectification rights. An earlier Austrian complaint was transferred to the Irish DPC — [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/violent-chatgpt-hallucination-sparks-gdpr-complaint-a-27788); [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/chatgpt-hallucinates-a-man-murdered-his-kids-openai-faces-a-complaint-in-europe-it-shouldnt-ignore/).
- **EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (EU, December 2024).** Covers (i) when AI models are anonymous, (ii) legitimate interest as a legal basis, and (iii) the effect of unlawfully processed training data. Legitimate interest "cannot be the 'by default' legal basis" and needs a three-step test, with mitigations such as pseudonymisation — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models). Models trained on personal data cannot be presumed anonymous — [Syrenis](https://syrenis.com/resources/blog/ai-models-under-gdpr/).
- **CJEU EDPS v SRB, C-413/23 P (EU, September 4, 2025).** Pseudonymised data can be personal data for the controller but not for a recipient that cannot re-identify it. Personal opinions are personal data. Transparency about recipients is judged from the controller's viewpoint at the time of collection — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/eu-the-srb-decision-a-new-era-for-personal-data-and-data-processing-agreements); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/rethinking-personal-data-the-cjeus-contextual-turn-in-edps-vs-srb/); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/09/pseudonymized-data-after-edps-v-srb.html). The EDPB's draft Pseudonymisation Guidelines 1/2025 take a more restrictive line — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2025/09/cjeu-clarifies-scope-of-personal-data-in-edps-v-srb-decision) (via search summary).

### Inferences
- **"Deleted" is a policy, not a guarantee.** A US court can override a provider's deletion promises worldwide, apart from geographies or contracts the order carves out. A B2B bot's privacy notice should say "deleted within X days, unless we are legally required to retain", and the provider contract (ZDR or enterprise tier) is what reduces this exposure.
- **Discovery reaches chat logs.** Logs already held were sampled, de-identified and produced. Anything the ICP bot logs (transcripts, extracted slots) could be discoverable in litigation involving the operator or its LLM provider, which is an argument for short retention and minimisation.
- **Venue matters.** For an EU-facing bot operated from outside the EU, the Garante annulment shows the one-stop-shop forum depends on having a recognised EU main establishment. Without one, any EU DPA can act, as the Garante did in 2023–2024.
- **Pseudonymise before sending to the LLM.** SRB gives some legal support for pseudonymising data before it reaches a sub-processor. The EDPB's stricter draft guidance means this should be treated as risk reduction, not as a way out of GDPR obligations.

## 3. Memory security: prompt-injection that writes false or persistent memories, exfiltration through connectors, browsing and files, and shared-link indexing

### Takeaway
From 2024 to early 2026, researchers repeatedly showed that untrusted content (web pages, documents, emails, shared Drive files) can write instructions into ChatGPT's long-term memory. Those instructions persist across sessions and, combined with image or URL rendering, leak data continuously. OpenAI fixed specific exfiltration channels, but memory writes triggered by injected content were not fixed at the root. Separately, the July 2025 "discoverable" share-link experiment (about 4,500 chats indexed by Google) and the August 2025 Grok share-link exposure (370,000+ chats) showed that sharing features can turn private chats into public, searchable pages.

### Cited Findings
- **SpAIware (Rehberger, Embrace The Red, Sept 20, 2024).** "Through prompt injection from untrusted data, attackers could insert long-term persistent spyware into ChatGPT." Data left through an invisible image pointing at an attacker server, with user data in the query string, so "all new conversation going forward will contain the attackers instructions". Timeline: fake-memory injection reported May 2024 and closed as a model-safety issue; end-to-end exploit reported June 2024; fixed in ChatGPT 1.2024.247 in September 2024. Root cause not fixed: "A website or untrusted document can still invoke the memory tool to store arbitrary memories." User advice was to review memories regularly, delete suspicious ones, and use Temporary Chat — [Embrace The Red](https://embracethered.com/blog/posts/2024/chatgpt-macos-app-persistent-data-exfiltration/).
- **SpAIware paper.** Published as a peer-reviewed paper by Herrador and Rehberger in *Future Generation Computer Systems* — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167739X25002894).
- **"Month of AI Bugs" (August 2025).** During this series Rehberger reportedly showed that ChatGPT's allow-listed image domains could be abused to leak chat history and stored memories — [LastPass blog (secondary)](https://blog.lastpass.com/posts/prompt-injection) (via search summary; not checked against primary posts).
- **Tenable "HackedGPT" (November 2025).** Seven ChatGPT flaws, including "Persistent Memory Injection" (instructions stored in memory "causing ongoing data leaks until the stored memory is cleared"); conversation injection via SearchGPT browsing; and markdown/code-block rendering bugs that hide commands from users. Attacks can be 0-click or 1-click. Some issues were still unpatched at publication, including in GPT-5. Tenable recommends isolating browsing, search and memory features — [SecurityBrief UK](https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/seven-chatgpt-flaws-expose-user-data-to-attack-tenable-warns); [iTWire](https://itwire.com/guest-articles/guest-research/seven-critical-vulnerabilities-open-chatgpt-to-data-theft-and-hijacking).
- **Zenity "AgentFlayer" (Black Hat USA, August 2025).** A poisoned document hides instructions in tiny white text. Once it lands in a victim's Google Drive, for example by being shared, a routine request through ChatGPT Connectors triggers a search for API keys, which are sent out via image-render URLs. The work was a proof of concept with no confirmed customer harm — [CSO Online](https://csoonline.com/article/4036868/black-hat-researchers-demonstrate-zero-click-prompt-injection-attacks-in-popular-ai-agents.html); [Vibe Graveyard](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/chatgpt-connectors-agentflayer-zero-click-exfiltration/).
- **Radware "ShadowLeak" (2025).** A single email could hijack ChatGPT's Deep Research agent through the Gmail connector. OpenAI marked it resolved on September 3, 2025 — [The Record](https://therecord.media/openai-fixes-zero-click-shadowleak-vulnerability).
- **Radware "ZombieAgent" (disclosed January 2026).** Zero-click via Connectors (Gmail, Outlook, Drive, GitHub). It plants malicious rules in long-term memory so the attack keeps running without the attacker, and leaks data one character at a time through pre-built URLs to get around OpenAI's link-modification defence — [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4115110/zombieagent-chatgpt-attack-shows-persistent-data-leak-risks-of-ai-agents.html); [The Register, "bandaids on bandaids" (Jan 8, 2026)](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/08/openai_chatgpt_prompt_injection/); [KBI Media press release](https://kbi.media/press-release/radware-reveals-zombieagent-zero-click-vulnerability-enabling-silent-ai-agent-takeover-and-data-exfiltration/). The patch date is reported as December 16, 2025 by [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/this-zombieagent-zero-click-vulnerability-allows-for-silent-account-takeover-heres-what-we-know).
- **ChatGPT shared-link indexing (late July – early August 2025).** Shared chats were indexed when users ticked a "discoverable" option. Fast Company counted more than 4,500 indexed links, some containing PII. On July 31, 2025, OpenAI CISO Dane Stuckey announced the feature's removal and called it "a short-lived experiment". Some content stayed visible on Bing and DuckDuckGo until they recrawled. Google said publishers control indexing — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/openai-is-pulling-shared-chatgpt-chats-from-google-search/552671/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-google-search-indexes/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/openai-pulls-chat-sharing-tool-after-google-search-privacy-scare).
- **Grok share-link exposure (August 2025, industry comparator).** More than 370,000 Grok conversations were indexed by Google, Bing and DuckDuckGo. Clicking "share" published the conversation "without warning or a disclaimer". Exposed content included personal details, at least one password, and uploaded files such as images and spreadsheets — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/xai-published-hundreds-of-thousands-of-grok-chatbot-conversations/); [Yahoo/Fortune](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/thousands-private-user-conversations-elon-173216941.html).
- **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025.** Sensitive Information Disclosure moved up to LLM02. New entries include LLM07 System Prompt Leakage (do not put secrets in system prompts; enforce controls outside the model) and LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses (mitigations include permission-aware vector stores and logging retrieval activity) — [Coralogix](https://coralogix.com/ai-blog/owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications/); [Bright Security](https://brightsec.com/blog/owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-in-2025/) (secondary summaries of OWASP).

### Inferences
- **Memory writes are a privileged action.** Any memory or profile store the ICP bot keeps (for example ICP slots carried across sessions) should only be written from first-party user turns. Content that is retrieved, browsed, uploaded or pulled through a connector should never trigger a memory write. If it is allowed to, require explicit user confirmation and show the user what is being saved.
- **Output rendering is an exfiltration channel.** Markdown images, auto-fetched links, and URLs with dynamic query strings are how almost every exploit above got data out. A lead-qualification widget should render plain text or sanitised markdown with no remote images and no attacker-controllable URLs.
- **Retrieved content is untrusted.** In a B2B bot that retrieves prospect websites, CRM records or uploaded decks, that material should be treated like any other untrusted input, as OWASP LLM01/LLM08 describe. Isolate it from the instruction context and from memory, as Tenable recommends.
- **Sharing features need safe defaults.** Transcripts should never be public by default, share links should carry noindex headers and expire, and the UI should warn plainly before anything is shared. Both indexing incidents came from weak warnings plus search-engine crawlability.
- **Assume the system prompt will leak.** Keep scoring rubrics, API keys, CRM tokens and other customers' data out of it (OWASP LLM07).

## 4. Regulation: GDPR, EU AI Act chatbot transparency and timelines, CCPA/CPRA, US state chatbot laws

### Takeaway
**EU:** AI Act Article 50(1) chatbot disclosure has applied since **August 2, 2026**. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, in force July 27, 2026) postponed the high-risk obligations but, according to the commentary found, did not move Art. 50(1). Under GDPR, regulators expect a documented lawful basis (legitimate interest is "not default"), workable erasure and rectification (output filters are not erasure, per the ICO), and accuracy. **US:** California SB 243 (companion chatbots, in force January 1, 2026) probably does not reach a B2B lead bot because of its customer-service exclusion. General bot-disclosure laws do reach it: Maine (2025), Utah (on request or high-risk), and from January 1, 2027 California AB 1609 for businesses with more than $500M revenue. The CCPA's new regulations bring risk assessments (from January 1, 2026) and ADMT rules (from January 1, 2027).

### Cited Findings
**EU AI Act (EU)**
- **Art. 50(1).** "Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed" so that people know they are interacting with AI, unless this is "obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed". **Art. 50(5):** the information must be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", clearly and accessibly — [AI Act Art. 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/).
- **Timeline after the Omnibus.** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published in the OJ on July 24, 2026 and entered into force July 27, 2026. "Article 50 transparency and AI content-marking still apply on 2 August 2026." Generative systems already on the market before then must meet Art. 50(2) marking by December 2, 2026. High-risk deadlines moved to December 2, 2027 (Annex III) and August 2, 2028 (Annex I) — [Legalithm (vendor blog)](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026); [Technology.org (Oct 13, 2026)](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/) (title and search summary; page returned 403). One vendor source (Usercentrics) had contradictory wording on whether Art. 50 was delayed — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/).
- **Commission guidance.** The Commission adopted final Article 50 guidelines on July 20, 2026 and judged the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content adequate — [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026). The guidelines reportedly treat complaints handling as a sensitive domain where periodic reminders, beyond a first-contact notice, are likely needed — [CMSWire / search summary](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/is-your-contact-center-ready-for-europes-chatbot-disclosure-rules/).
- **Penalties.** Up to €15M or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher (Art. 99(4)) — [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026).

**GDPR (EU/EEA) and UK GDPR (UK)**
- **Lawful basis (EU).** EDPB Opinion 28/2024: legitimate interest "cannot be the 'by default' legal basis" for training or using AI models. It needs a three-step necessity and balancing test, with mitigations — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models).
- **Enforcement findings (Italy).** The Garante's grounds against OpenAI were breach notification, training lawful basis, transparency and age verification. That decision was annulled on jurisdiction, not on the merits — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/italian-court-kills-openais-eur15m-fine-and-it-wasnt-even-close/). Replika: data minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)) and privacy by design (Art. 25(1)) breaches — [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/ai-the-italian-supervisory-authority-fines-company-behind-chatbot-replika_ga).
- **Erasure and rights (UK).** The ICO's December 12, 2024 outcomes report says output filters do not amount to deleting data from a model, criticises developers for not enabling information rights, and asks controllers to design for individual rights from the outset — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/ico-publish-outcome-report-on-ai-consultation-series); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/ico-updates-its-views-using-personal-data-generative-ai-uk).
- **Accuracy and rectification (EU/EEA).** The noyb Norway complaint argues that disclaimers do not meet accuracy and rectification duties — [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/violent-chatgpt-hallucination-sparks-gdpr-complaint-a-27788).

**California (US-CA)**
- **SB 243 (companion chatbots).** Signed October 13, 2025, effective January 1, 2026. Requires disclosure, safety protocols, and break reminders at least every 3 hours for known minors. Annual reporting starts July 1, 2027. Private right of action for the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation. Exclusions include customer service bots — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/california-companion-chatbot-law-now-effect); [Gunderson](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-california-sb-243-new-compliance-requirements-for-operators-of-ai-companion-chatbots).
- **AB 1609 (Right to Human Customer Service Act).** Signed September 28, 2026, effective January 1, 2027. Applies to businesses with more than $500M annual gross revenue serving California customers. They must disclose an AI customer-service chatbot where a customer is likely to mistake it for a human, must not present AI as human, and must make a good-faith effort to connect to a human within 15 minutes. Penalties are reported at $5,000 for a first violation and $10,000 after that — [Governor's office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/28/governor-newsom-signs-commonsense-legislation-to-make-your-life-easier/); [Fox Rothschild](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2026/09/articles/general-privacy-data-security-news-developments/5118/); [Assembly analysis](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2026-04/ab-1609-zbur-apcp-analysis.pdf).
- **CCPA/CPRA regulations.** Approved September 2025, effective January 1, 2026. Risk assessments start January 1, 2026, with attestations and summaries due to the CPPA by April 1, 2028. ADMT obligations for "significant decisions" start January 1, 2027. Cybersecurity audit certifications run 2028–2030 depending on revenue — [CPPA announcement](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/newly-approved-ccpa-regulations-have-staggered-deadlines-for-compliance). Processing that was already under way before 2026 reportedly has until December 31, 2027 to complete assessments — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/newly-approved-ccpa-regulations-have-staggered-deadlines-for-compliance).

**Other US states**
- **New York (US-NY), companion law.** Requires conspicuous notice every three hours that the user is not talking to a human. AG enforcement only, no private right of action. Reported effective date November 5, 2025 — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai); [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/01/analyzing-the-new-ai-companion-chatbot-laws/).
- **Maine (US-ME).** Effective September 2025. Anyone using a chatbot in trade or commerce must clearly and conspicuously disclose that the consumer is not dealing with a human whenever the bot could mislead. Enforced under the Unfair Trade Practices Act — [Wiley](https://www.wileyconnect.com/state-chatbot-laws-are-moving-from-transparency-to-risk-management); [search summary of state guides](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-85-state-ai-chatbot-regulation-laws).
- **Utah (US-UT).** AI Policy Act (SB 149, amended by SB 226). Disclosure is required when a user asks, or in high-risk interactions (health, financial, biometric). $2,500 per violation — [Beancount guide (secondary)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/19/ai-chatbot-human-disclosure-laws-state-guide); [Wiley](https://www.wileyconnect.com/state-chatbot-laws-are-moving-from-transparency-to-risk-management).
- **Overall landscape.** Disclosure laws in Utah, California, Colorado and Maine; companion laws in California and New York; mental-health AI limits in Illinois, Nevada and Utah — [MultiState (Jan 2026)](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-85-state-ai-chatbot-regulation-laws).

### Inferences
- **Disclose AI everywhere.** For an ICP/lead bot serving EU users, an "you are chatting with an AI assistant" notice at first interaction is mandatory from August 2, 2026 (EU). The same notice also satisfies Maine (US-ME), Utah-on-request (US-UT) and AB 1609 (US-CA, large businesses, from 2027). The cheapest compliant design is to always disclose at session start and never claim to be human.
- **SB 243 is probably out of scope.** A B2B lead bot is unlikely to be a "companion chatbot" under SB 243 (US-CA), since it is not relationship-building and falls under the customer-service exclusion. If the bot adds persona, memory of personal life details, or "relationship" features, that should be re-checked.
- **ADMT exposure.** If ICP scoring drives automated decisions about individuals, for example auto-rejecting a lead or setting pricing or terms, the CCPA ADMT rules (US-CA, from January 2027) and GDPR Art. 22 (EU) could apply. Scoring companies rather than people, and keeping a human in the loop, lowers that risk.
- **Rights architecture.** Under GDPR and UK GDPR, erasure of chat-derived memory must be real deletion from stores and indexes. Prompt-level suppression is not enough, per the ICO. Do not fine-tune on user conversations unless erasure can be honoured, which in practice means not training on them at all.

## 5. B2B-specific concerns: third-party PII, confidential company data, sub-processor disclosure to OpenAI/Anthropic, provider retention defaults, DPAs

### Takeaway
In B2B chat, users routinely paste customer, employee and prospect data (Harmonic found 8.5% of enterprise prompts contained sensitive data, and 45.8% of that was customer data). An ICP bot will therefore collect third-party PII and confidential commercial data that the operator never collected directly. The ChatGPT ecosystem shows two risks in the sub-processor chain: an analytics vendor breach (Mixpanel, November 2025) exposed API customers' names and emails, and LLM providers keep about 30 days of API data by default unless ZDR is contracted. Both have to appear in DPAs, sub-processor lists and privacy notices.

### Cited Findings
- **Sensitive data in prompts.** Of enterprise prompts studied (2024 data), 8.5% contained sensitive information, and within that subset 45.8% potentially disclosed customer data and 26.8% employee data — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/01/20/genai-prompts-risk/); [Harmonic Security](https://www.harmonic.security/blog-posts/genai-in-the-enterprise-its-getting-personal). In Q3 2025, 26.4% of file uploads to GenAI tools contained sensitive data, up from 22% in Q2 — [BusinessWire / Harmonic](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251113987633/en/Over-a-Quarter-26-of-Uploads-to-GenAI-Tools-Contain-Sensitive-Data-an-Increase-of-Over-4-in-Just-Three-Months). Caveat: vendor research based on security-conscious enterprises.
- **Self-reported behaviour.** In a January 2025 TELUS Digital survey, 57% of 1,000 US employees said they had entered confidential information into AI platforms — [Dark Reading / search summary](https://www.darkreading.com/threat-intelligence/employees-sensitive-data-genai-prompts).
- **Sub-processor breach: OpenAI / Mixpanel (disclosed November 26, 2025).** An attacker reached Mixpanel through a smishing campaign that began November 8. Mixpanel detected it November 9 and shared the dataset with OpenAI November 25. Exposed: API account name and email, approximate city/state/country, OS and browser, referring sites, and user IDs. Not exposed: chats, API keys, credentials, payment data and government IDs. OpenAI stopped using Mixpanel. A December 19 clarification added that "a limited number of ChatGPT users" were also affected — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/mixpanel-incident/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/27/openai_mixpanel_api/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/11/27/mixpanel-breach-compromises-openai-api-users-account-data/); [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/openai-discloses-api-user-data-breach-via-third-party-mixpanel/).
- **Provider retention defaults.** OpenAI API: about 30 days for abuse monitoring, with ZDR for eligible endpoints on approval; stateful features are not ZDR-eligible — [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/). Anthropic API: backend deletion within 30 days, with exceptions for ZDR agreements, Usage Policy enforcement (flagged content up to 2 years) and legal requirements — [Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996866-how-long-do-you-store-my-organization-s-data).
- **Legal-hold exposure by tier.** In the NYT matter, Enterprise and Edu customers were exempt, while consumer and standard (non-ZDR) API traffic was most exposed — [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/).
- **DPAs.** OpenAI Enterprise customers can get a DPA — [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/does-chatgpt-store-your-data/). Anthropic commercial data is not used for training by default, and the 5-year consumer retention does not apply to commercial tiers — [Anarlog (secondary)](https://anarlog.so/blog/anthropic-data-retention-policy/).
- **Pseudonymised data sent to processors (EU).** The CJEU (SRB, September 2025) held that pseudonymised data may not be personal data for a recipient that cannot re-identify it, but transparency about recipients is still owed by the controller at collection — [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/rethinking-personal-data-the-cjeus-contextual-turn-in-edps-vs-srb/).

### Inferences
- **Roles.** The ICP bot operator is likely a processor when it acts for a client tenant collecting that client's prospect or ICP data, and a controller for its own analytics. In both cases OpenAI or Anthropic is a (sub-)processor. That chain has to be in the DPA, with a named sub-processor list, region, retention and change-notification terms (GDPR Art. 28; EU). This is GDPR-text reasoning, not from a fetched source.
- **Third-party PII is collected indirectly.** When a user names a prospect ("our champion is Jane Doe, jane@acme.com"), that person's data has been collected without their involvement. GDPR Art. 14 (EU) transparency, and the risk of keeping it in memory, point toward three controls: (a) discourage or redact individual-level PII in ICP interviews, which are about firmographics, roles and pains, not named people; (b) never write third-party names or emails into long-term memory; (c) apply short retention to raw transcripts.
- **Product analytics count.** The Mixpanel case shows that analytics, session-replay and error-tracking tools are sub-processors too. The bot should not send transcript text or PII to them, and they belong in the sub-processor list.
- **Confidential company data.** Pricing, pipeline numbers and customer lists can arrive in ICP answers. The DPA should promise no training on that data and set retention; provider ZDR or an enterprise tier narrows legal-hold and retention exposure.

## 6. Practical controls: PII redaction before logging, encryption, retention windows, consent UX, an audit trail of memory use, and a "forget me" flow

### Takeaway
Every control on the list maps to a documented ChatGPT-era failure or regulatory expectation. Redact before logging (prompts routinely contain customer PII). Keep retention short and enforce it (court holds and discovery reach whatever is stored). Gate memory writes and show them (SpAIware, HackedGPT, ZombieAgent). Use private-by-default sharing (ChatGPT and Grok indexing). Disclose AI at first contact (AI Act Art. 50, Maine, AB 1609). Make erasure real and verifiable across chats, memory and derived data (ICO, noyb, GDPR).

### Cited Findings
- **Users can't see what memory holds.** Rehberger's advice to "regularly review the memories the system stores" and delete suspicious entries assumes users can see and edit memory — [Embrace The Red](https://embracethered.com/blog/posts/2024/chatgpt-macos-app-persistent-data-exfiltration/). Tenable notes that injected memories cause "ongoing data leaks until the stored memory is cleared" — [SecurityBrief UK](https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/seven-chatgpt-flaws-expose-user-data-to-attack-tenable-warns).
- **Isolate features.** Tenable recommends isolating browsing, search and memory to prevent cross-context attacks — [SecurityBrief UK](https://securitybrief.co.uk/story/seven-chatgpt-flaws-expose-user-data-to-attack-tenable-warns).
- **Log retrieval.** OWASP LLM08 mitigations include permission-aware vector stores, data validation pipelines and logging of retrieval activity. LLM07 says to keep sensitive data out of system prompts and enforce controls outside the model — [Coralogix](https://coralogix.com/ai-blog/owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications/).
- **Design for rights.** The ICO (UK) says output filters are not erasure and asks controllers to design for information rights from the outset — [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/ico-publish-outcome-report-on-ai-consultation-series).
- **Memory deletion is separate from chat deletion.** In ChatGPT, memory persists separately from chats, and turning off reference chat history triggers deletion within 30 days — [WitnessAI](https://witness.ai/blog/ai-data-retention/).
- **Legal holds survive deletion.** Retained logs were preserved under a hold and later sampled and produced (20M de-identified logs) — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-no-longer-has-to-preserve-all-of-its-chatgpt-data-with-some-exceptions-192422093.html); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright).
- **Share-link exposure.** Exposure came from opt-in checkboxes users did not understand (ChatGPT) and from no warning at all (Grok) — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/openai-is-pulling-shared-chatgpt-chats-from-google-search/552671/); [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/xai-published-hundreds-of-thousands-of-grok-chatbot-conversations/).
- **Exfiltration via rendering.** Image and URL rendering was the exfiltration channel in SpAIware, AgentFlayer and ZombieAgent — [Embrace The Red](https://embracethered.com/blog/posts/2024/chatgpt-macos-app-persistent-data-exfiltration/); [CSO Online](https://csoonline.com/article/4036868/black-hat-researchers-demonstrate-zero-click-prompt-injection-attacks-in-popular-ai-agents.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/08/openai_chatgpt_prompt_injection/).
- **Encryption baseline.** OpenAI's enterprise-privacy page (not fetchable in this session) is commonly cited for AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.2+ in transit, but I could not verify it here. See Gaps.
